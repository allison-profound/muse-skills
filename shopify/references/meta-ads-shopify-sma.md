# Connect and onboard Shopify Meta App

- On a confidential VM, this setup is unavailable, even with consent. Do not run any `shopify meta-app` command there, including `status`. Explain that connecting a Shopify store to Facebook and Instagram is not available on this Muse, and that nothing changed. A confidential VM is one whose runtime facts say `vm=Confidential`; `vm=Secure` is not confidential, and this setup works there. On every VM, confidential or not, do not write the store, the chosen business portfolio, catalog or dataset, any status or error, or setup progress to memory or files; they are live state, so read the status fresh each time.

- Set up Meta's Facebook & Instagram app on a merchant's Shopify store: settle the task, check what exists, agree on a plan, install, onboard, then verify. These `shopify meta-app` commands are the only way to do it. They work whether or not the store is connected to Muse, and they are not Shopify tools, so they do not appear in `list-tools`.

- When you talk to the merchant, call the business a business portfolio, including when you ask which one to connect the store to. Tool names, flags and fields still say business (`ads_catalog_get_businesses`, `--business-id`, `business_id`); do not show them to the merchant.

```sh
/opt/hatch/bin/shopify meta-app status --website <storefront or *.myshopify.com address>
/opt/hatch/bin/shopify meta-app install --shop-domain <store>.myshopify.com --business-id <id>
/opt/hatch/bin/shopify meta-app onboard --shop-domain <store>.myshopify.com --business-id <id> --catalog-id <id> [--pixel-id <id>]
```

- Business portfolios, catalogs, datasets and their events are read and created with the tools in [Meta Business Manager](/opt/hatch/connectors/meta_ads/CONNECTOR.md), such as `ads_catalog_get_businesses` and `ads_catalog_create`.

## Settle the task

- Work out whether the merchant wants a new connection, a repair of an existing one, or a first sales ad, and what business result they want. Do not swap a sales goal for traffic or link clicks without telling them. When they want a sales ad and the store is not connected yet, the connection comes first: after saying so, ask only the next setup question, whether to connect the store or which business portfolio to use, and save the ad account, products, budget, schedule and audience for ad creation.

- When the store is already connected and working, say so first, before anything about ad accounts or existing campaigns: one line that the Facebook & Instagram app is installed and authorized, then the business portfolio, catalog and dataset it uses, read with `ads_catalog_get_businesses`, `ads_catalog_list_catalogs` and `ads_get_datasets`, and name any read that returned no data as unchecked. Then, when the merchant wants a sales ad or more sales, move on to the campaign; when they only asked whether the store is connected, end with that result. Do not report existing campaigns unless the merchant asks about them, and never attribute a read's results to an ad account other than the `ad_account_id` the result itself names. In the connection line, name only the business portfolio, catalog and dataset the status returns; the ad account is not part of the store connection. This is not the after-onboarding report: do not offer the delivery check or close with its next-step sentence. When moving on to the campaign, ask whether to start the sales ad with the confirmed store, business portfolio, catalog and dataset. Run the dataset and catalog checks in [diagnosis](/opt/hatch/connectors/meta_ads/references/signals-connections-diagnosis.md) before you ask it or end with the result, or, if you do not, report product sync and events on their own lines as pending (not checked yet).

- Identify the store first. Use a store or website the merchant already named in the conversation, or one an earlier turn already named back to them that they went along with, such as by agreeing to set it up; then do not ask about the store again, and go straight to the business portfolio question. Otherwise ask once for the store's website or `*.myshopify.com` address, unless the Shopify connector is already connected: run `shopify status`, and when it shows a connected store, run `shopify call-tool --name get-shop-info` and ask one yes/no question instead, whether the store connected to Muse, named by its `*.myshopify.com` address, is the one they mean, as its own message: do not ask about or recommend a business portfolio in the same message. You can still say what the merchant's goal needs, such as a connected store for a sales ad, and note any budget they gave as a limit for later. The connected store can be a different store of theirs, a client's or a sample store, so confirm it, and ask for the address when they say no or no `*.myshopify.com` domain comes back. This setup does not need the Shopify connector, so never ask them to connect it. When you ask for the address, say you need it to check Meta's Facebook & Instagram app on that store.

- Use the confirmed store for every `shopify meta-app` command.

- Keep this job to the connection. Product edits, themes, fulfillment, inventory and non-Shopify data sources belong to other skills.

## Inspect what already exists

- Read the store's Facebook & Instagram app installation status by running `shopify meta-app status` with the confirmed store's `*.myshopify.com` domain or website before recommending anything. The Muse Shopify connector is a different integration, so a connected Shopify store says nothing about the Facebook & Instagram app.

- `confirmed_shopify: false` means no Shopify store was identified for the website. That is unknown, not "not Shopify". Ask whether the store runs on Shopify and, if it does, for its `*.myshopify.com` address, in one question, then check again. When the merchant named that website, keep working on it: ask whether it runs on Shopify and, if so, for its `*.myshopify.com` address, and mention a different store connected to Muse only as context, not as a replacement to choose. Never derive or guess a `*.myshopify.com` address from the website or the store's name; a status for an address the merchant did not give does not show the store is theirs.

- Treat the installation as installed only when its status is `ACCESS_TOKEN_DEPOSITED`. `TEMPORARILY_UNAVAILABLE` means it needs reauthorizing, including when its stored token stopped working, and a null status means it is not installed. When the merchant says they installed it but the status is null, say Meta does not show it installed yet, without guessing why. `UNINSTALLED` means the merchant removed the app: say it was removed and treat it as not installed. `ATTEMPTED_INSTALL` means an install was started but not approved: treat it as not installed. When it needs reauthorizing, tell the merchant they reauthorize it by opening a new install link that you create for the business portfolio they choose, so the business portfolio question comes with its reason. `shop_domain` is the store's `*.myshopify.com` address for the other two commands. Tell the merchant the state in plain words, such as installed, needs reauthorizing or not installed, rather than the raw status value. When `status` itself fails, including when it says this step is not available for the account, the installation is unknown: say you could not check it, and never say whether the store is or is not connected. Do not add that the store is not connected after saying nothing changed.

- Business portfolio, pixel, catalog and data-sharing level come back only when the merchant's linked Facebook account can access the installation. A missing value or an access error is unknown, never absent or healthy, so do not reinstall or reconnect because of it. The status returns them as IDs, so read their names with Meta Business Manager (`ads_catalog_get_businesses`, `ads_catalog_list_catalogs`, `ads_get_datasets`) and tell the merchant the names. When `status` fails with `SHOPIFY_LOGIN_REQUIRED`, the merchant needs to log in to Shopify before the store can be checked, so ask them to and check again when they say they have. Until then, do not create another install link, start onboarding or treat the app as installed. When the merchant says they already logged in and the status still asks for a Shopify login, do not ask them to log in again. Say you still can't read or connect the store from this account and that nothing changed, and say what they can check: which Facebook account is linked here, or ask the store owner or the business portfolio's admin for access. You may create one install link for the chosen business portfolio to confirm; when its error is `SHOP_NOT_ACCESSIBLE`, say so and stop.

- An installed app can still have no business portfolio, catalog or dataset connected. When the status returns the installation's business portfolio, the merchant can see its connections, so a missing catalog or dataset means it is not connected. That is an incomplete connection: say the app is installed and connected to that business portfolio, then name every connection that is missing, each on its own with what it means, such as products not syncing without a catalog and purchases not shared without a dataset, before asking any choice; keep that business portfolio, do not reinstall or share an install link because connections are missing, and continue with the catalog and dataset choices. When no business portfolio comes back either, say the app is installed but not connected to a business portfolio, catalog or dataset yet, read each business portfolio's catalogs with `ads_catalog_list_catalogs` and its datasets with `ads_get_datasets`, and only after both reads return for every business portfolio you list, say what each one has and that the repair will reuse the chosen business portfolio's existing catalog and dataset rather than create new ones; never say a business portfolio has no catalog or dataset unless its read returned none, and start with the business portfolio choice. Either way, the repair finishes with `shopify meta-app onboard`, not `install`.

- Find the merchant's existing business portfolio, ad account, datasets and catalogs with Meta Business Manager. Prefer a compatible existing dataset and catalog over creating new ones.

- Look up facts together, but ask one unresolved choice at a time: which store, which business portfolio, which dataset, which catalog and what data sharing.

## Decide which business portfolio, catalog, and (optionally) which dataset the advertiser would like to connect to Shopify.

- Before the first choice, tell the merchant that connecting the store means linking Meta's Facebook & Instagram app on their Shopify store to a business portfolio, a catalog and optionally a dataset. The business portfolio owns the connection. The store's products sync into the catalog so ads can show them. The dataset tracks customer activity on the store, such as purchases, which can help show ads to people more likely to buy and measure the sales ads bring in.

- When the installation status already returned the business portfolio, that business portfolio is settled, since onboarding cannot move the store to another one: name it, do not ask the advertiser to choose one, and go straight to the catalog, or ask only about the dataset when the catalog is connected too. Otherwise, clarifying which business portfolio the advertiser is trying to connect to Shopify is the most important piece here, and that should come first. List every business portfolio the business portfolio lookup (`ads_catalog_get_businesses`) returned, as a list or options, not only the recommended one and not from the ad accounts, since a business portfolio can have no ad account yet. Give the advertiser the option to name another business portfolio, since the list may not show every business portfolio they can use. Mark one business portfolio as recommended when the evidence points to it, such as a business portfolio whose name matches the store, or one that already owns the store's ad account, catalog or dataset, and say why. When nothing points to one, list them without a recommendation. Ask only which business portfolio; mentioning what it owns explains the recommendation, but leave the catalog and the dataset for their own questions after the business portfolio is settled.

- Once the business portfolio is settled, ask the advertiser to choose a catalog as its own question: list the business portfolio's catalogs from the catalog lookup, recommend one that already belongs to the business portfolio with the reason, and offer creating a new one. Ask it even when the business portfolio has only one catalog, since creating a new one is always an option. Do not offer to name a different catalog: unlike a business portfolio, the catalog must belong to the chosen business portfolio, and the lookup already lists those. When the merchant names a catalog themselves, look it up; when it belongs to another business portfolio, say that using it would mean moving the store to that business portfolio and leaving the current connection, and ask whether they want that or to keep the current setup. Do not fold the dataset or the plan approval into the same message.

- Finally, ask the advertiser whether they would like to connect a dataset, and say what it is for: it tracks customer activity on the store, which can help show ads to people more likely to buy. Unlike catalog and business portfolio, this step is optional. Similarly to catalog and business portfolio, list out available datasets and give the advertiser the opportunity to specify a different one or to create a new one. Recommend a dataset that already belongs to the business portfolio, with the reason, and say they can skip this step for now.

- After these three selections have been made, show the full plan: which assets will be connected to the Facebook & Instagram app on their store, and what each one does. Then ask for the advertiser's approval for the complete plan. Do not offer a one-tap approval of business portfolio, catalog and dataset together, even when one option is clearly right, including when repairing a failed setup: ask each choice on its own first. When the app is not installed yet, the plan says the merchant approves the app in Shopify through the install link.

- Reuse the existing setup when it is correct. Repair an incomplete connection by onboarding the business portfolio's existing catalog and dataset instead of creating new ones, never send an install link for an app that is already installed, unless onboarding returns `INSTALL_NOT_APPROVED`, and ask before replacing a catalog or dataset the merchant chose on purpose.

## Install or authorize the app

- When the app is not installed or needs reauthorizing, create an install link by running `shopify meta-app install` for the store's `*.myshopify.com` address and the chosen business portfolio, and share the returned `install_url` for the merchant to open in their browser. The same link opens the app when it is already installed. Errors start with a type, such as `BUSINESS_ACCESS_DENIED`. The type tells you what happened; never show it, a status code or the raw error text to the merchant, and say what it means in plain words. `BUSINESS_ACCESS_DENIED` means the merchant's Facebook account is not in the business portfolio, so ask which business portfolio to use instead, as the business portfolio question describes: the business portfolios you can see, a recommendation, and the option to name another business portfolio. Keep the name-another option even when you also say how to regain access to the denied business portfolio. `BUSINESS_ADMIN_REQUIRED` means connecting the store needs full control of the business portfolio, so ask them to have an admin of the business portfolio connect it or give them full control. Also offer to use another business portfolio they can see instead, such as the recommended one, without switching on your own. `SHOP_NOT_ACCESSIBLE` means the store is connected to a business portfolio the merchant cannot access, so say so and change nothing.

- The merchant approves the app in Shopify, outside Muse. Never ask for their Shopify password, two-factor code or token, in chat or through a login card such as `credentials.request_login`, and never log in to their Shopify admin, install the app or approve it with the browser, and never sign in for them: approving the app happens in their own browser through the install link. When they offer a password, tell them not to share it, and help them open the install link themselves, such as logging in to their Shopify admin in their own browser first, or creating a new install link. Do not promise the whole flow happens inside chat.

- After sharing the link for an app that is not installed or needs reauthorizing, run `shopify meta-app status --website <store>.myshopify.com --wait-until-installed` with `background: true`, so setup continues once the merchant approves the app without them having to come back and say so. It checks every 15 seconds for up to 30 minutes and finishes with `wait` set to `installed` or `timed_out`. Tell the merchant you will pick it up within a minute of their approval and what comes next, and that they can tap I'm finished if they are back sooner. After starting the check, call `muse.create_options` with the single option `I'm finished` and `button_style` `center_aligned_filled` as your last tool call, and place its `embed_token` in the same message as the link. If that background check returns `PROFESSIONAL_CONSENT_REQUIRED`, run `shopify meta-app status` once in the foreground so the consent card appears.

- When it finishes with `wait` set to `installed`, tell the merchant straight away that the app is installed and what you will connect next, naming the agreed business portfolio, catalog and dataset, and offer a single option to continue. The onboarding approval card appears under the merchant's latest message, so run `onboard` after they choose to continue. An installed app can still have no business portfolio, dataset, catalog or sharing connected, so use the returned status to see what is left.

- When it finishes with `timed_out`, or reports that it was interrupted, say you have not seen the installation yet and ask the merchant to tap I'm finished once they have approved the app in Shopify, with the button again.

- When the merchant taps I'm finished or says they are back, check the installation status again for the same store and carry on with the same task. When it now shows the app installed and the business portfolio, catalog and dataset are already agreed, run `onboard` with them right away, without offering a single option to continue first; its Confirm / Not now card is their consent. When it is still not installed, say so and share the same link with the button again. An installation result that arrives after you already carried on needs no reply.

- When any `shopify meta-app` command says connecting a Shopify store is not available for this account yet, stop the Shopify setup and say that connecting the store is not available for this account yet and that nothing changed. It means Muse cannot make Ads changes for this account right now, not that their Meta account lacks permissions, so do not call the account permanently ineligible. Do not suggest another way to connect the store, such as installing the app from Shopify Admin themselves, and do not plan or offer a campaign in that reply: wanting to advertise is not a request to create one. Only when the merchant then asks to create a campaign, plan it and use the Ads Manager path in [manual setup](/opt/hatch/connectors/meta_ads/references/campaign-manual-setup.md). A `connector_feature_unavailable` error is a different failure and does not mean this.

- When the merchant cannot or will not grant access, stop. Say which access is missing and who must do what, for example that someone who can install apps in their Shopify admin, such as the store owner, approves the Facebook & Instagram app there. Say that you made no changes, that the connection is unverified, and how to continue later.

## Initiate onboarding to connect the business portfolio, catalog, and dataset to Shopify

- After approval, start onboarding by running `shopify meta-app onboard` with `--business-id`, the chosen catalog as `--catalog-id` and the chosen dataset as `--pixel-id`. When the merchant chose a new catalog, create it with `ads_catalog_create` in the chosen business portfolio first and pass its ID. Leaving out the dataset skips the dataset step; it does not disconnect a dataset the store already shares with. `shopify meta-app onboard` kicks off an async job that takes around 41 seconds and finishes for most users within 70 seconds. Inform the advertiser that onboarding is in progress and that it should take about a minute. `SHOP_NOT_INSTALLED` means the app is not installed, so create an install link first. `INSTALL_NOT_APPROVED` means no install link for this business portfolio was approved on the store, for example because the app was installed outside Muse, so create a new link and ask a store admin to approve it. The app is already installed, so the installation check reports it installed before anyone approves the new link: do not start `--wait-until-installed` for this link or take an installed status as the approval. Share the link with the `I'm finished` button as above, wait for the merchant to say they finished approving it, then run `onboard` again with the agreed business portfolio, catalog and dataset, which shows the Confirm / Not now card again.

- Onboarding always shows the merchant a Confirm / Not now card naming Shopify as the partner, even after they agreed in chat or turned approvals off, and an earlier Allow never covers it. Do not describe the chat agreement as the consent. When they choose Not now, stop and do not retry. Say the requested Shopify connection or change was not made, and for a repair or switch that the last confirmed connection is unchanged, and if you created a catalog for this setup, say it remains in their business portfolio and that they can remove it in Commerce Manager. Do not delete it yourself.

- When `onboard` says loading the consent card failed, no card was shown and nothing changed. For a refusal (`BUSINESS_ADMIN_REQUIRED`, `BUSINESS_ACCESS_DENIED`, or `ASSET_NOT_ACCESSIBLE`, which means the same as `CATALOG_NOT_ACCESSIBLE` or `PIXEL_NOT_ACCESSIBLE` for the ID it names), relay it like the onboarding errors below. Do not run `onboard` again until the merchant fixes the cause or picks another business portfolio, catalog or dataset.

- When it failed on a timeout or server error, nothing changed. Run `onboard` again only if the merchant wants to; that shows the card again.

- Once onboard has returned a result, the merchant has already answered that card, so do not ask them to choose Confirm again.

- The command returns `installation_id` while onboarding runs in the background. Check the installation status: `onboarding_status` is `started` while onboarding runs, `succeeded` when it finished and `failed` when it did not, with the reason in `onboarding_error`. Wait between status reads in the foreground, with a time limit longer than the wait, so the reads finish within this turn. Report syncing as pending while it is `started`, rather than repeating the request. When it is `failed`, stop checking and explain the reason to the merchant in plain words. A failed onboarding can be retried automatically, so a later `started` means it is running again, but do not run `onboard` again without asking, because that raises a new card. Treat the business portfolio, catalog and dataset as connected only once their IDs appear.

- `SHOP_CONNECTED_TO_OTHER_BUSINESS` means stop and tell the merchant. Never move the store to another business portfolio yourself. `CATALOG_NOT_ACCESSIBLE` or `PIXEL_NOT_ACCESSIBLE` means the merchant's Facebook account cannot reach that catalog or dataset, so ask for another one. `BUSINESS_ACCESS_DENIED` and `BUSINESS_ADMIN_REQUIRED` mean the same as for the install link. Never show these types to the merchant either. After any of these, or when a check before onboarding shows the merchant cannot reach a chosen catalog or dataset, the requested change was not applied: say nothing changed; for an initial setup say the store is not connected yet, and for a repair or switch say the last confirmed connection is unchanged, reading the status again if it may be out of date; report product sync and events as pending (not checked yet), and never call the business portfolio or another chosen asset fine, connected, checked out or reachable, and never say you can reach it, even when a read shows the merchant can.

- After a timeout, an `INTERNAL_ERROR` or an unclear result, read the status before retrying, because the first attempt may have worked. Record each step that completed. On an `INTERNAL_ERROR` or an unclear result, say the connection is not confirmed, not that nothing changed, because a failed request may still have started onboarding. Even then, report app authorization, the connections, product sync and events as separate lines, each not confirmed or pending (not checked yet). When, after the merchant approved the setup, the status read itself fails with this internal error before onboarding, still run `shopify meta-app onboard` once with the agreed IDs, and report these separate lines only if that fails too; a status that says Shopify needs a login, that the step is not available for the account, or that the store or a chosen asset cannot be reached is not this error, so never onboard on it: follow what that status says.

- No supported operation removes an app, catalog or connection. When a step fails part-way, say exactly what changed and what the merchant can undo in Shopify Admin or Commerce Manager. Never claim a clean rollback you could not verify.

## Verify delivery

- After onboarding completes, report app authorization (the app is installed and authorized) and each connection the installation status confirms as separate results, then report product sync and events as two separate lines, each pending because it is not checked yet, without saying whether any have arrived (when no dataset was chosen, say this setup did not connect a dataset; when the status still returns one, report it as connected with its events not checked yet, and say purchase events are not shared only when the status returns no dataset; product sync is not checked yet), and do not say products are syncing before the sync is checked, and do not call the setup done or open by saying it is; say the connection is in place; then ask the merchant whether they want you to check that products reach the catalog and that events reach the dataset, when one was selected. When a dataset was selected, say in the same question that if it has not received events yet, checking means you visit their storefront, view and search products, and add items to the cart, and that the events this creates stay in their live data. In the same message, say that if they would rather not check now, product sync and events stay unverified and they can ask you to check any time. In this report only, while delivery is not checked yet and when the merchant's goal is a sales ad or more sales, close by saying that once delivery is checked or skipped, the next step is the sales ad, without asking about the ad yet.

- When they decline, restate app authorization and each confirmed connection in one line, then say product sync and events stay unverified and they can ask you to check any time, and do not call the store ready or working. When they accept, run the checks below and report each result. Do not record the connection or its status in memory.

- Check that the store's products appear in the catalog and that catalog diagnostics show no blocking errors.

- Check which events from the store the dataset is already receiving. When none have arrived yet and the merchant agreed to that storefront test, browse the storefront yourself to produce some: open pages, view and search products, and add items to the cart, whatever shows that events come through. Do not start checkout, pay, create an account, or submit the merchant's or anyone's personal details. If the storefront is password-protected, say that events cannot be checked until it is open, or ask the merchant to browse it themselves. When they did not agree to the storefront test, report events as pending instead of browsing.

- After browsing, read the dataset's events again. These events are real and stay in the merchant's live data, so tell the merchant which events came from your own visit, and never count them as shopper activity or sales.

- Report a real purchase as pending until one is received, never invent a conversion, and never ask the merchant to make a paid purchase to prove the setup.

- An installed app, a connected dataset or a setting alone does not prove that events are being delivered. When a check is unavailable, say it is pending, not passed.

- Report app authorization, connections, product sync and events received as separate lines, with Purchase events pending until a real order arrives, and name every read that returned no data as unchecked, and if you say how many, count exactly the reads you name, even when the merchant's next step is an ad. Start the report with the app line: whether the app is installed and authorized. Then offer an ongoing setup check without waiting to be asked, as its own decision, as Monitor setup health describes.

## Hand off to ad creation

- After a health check or delivery check confirms the connection is working and the merchant's goal is a sales ad or more sales, ask whether to start a sales ad with the confirmed store, business portfolio, catalog and dataset, as its own yes or no question alongside any monitoring offer; saying a sales ad is the next step is not the offer. When the merchant only asked whether the store or setup is working, the check's result is the answer: do not offer a sales ad. When the merchant says no, keep the setup result and note ad creation as their next step. If they ask you to save it for later, save only that they want a sales ad later, not the store, its connections or their status. Carry the confirmed setup into the ad: when exactly one ad account belongs to the connected business portfolio, use it and say which one, then ask only for the next missing input, one at a time, starting with which products to feature.

- When they accept, follow [Advertising Campaigns](/opt/muse/skills/advertising-campaigns/SKILL.md) and [campaign creation](/opt/hatch/connectors/meta_ads/references/campaign-creation.md). Carry forward the store website, business portfolio, ad account, catalog, dataset, Purchase evidence, the sales goal and any budget ceiling, so the merchant does not repeat them.

- When Purchase events are available and eligible, the sales recommendation should use Purchase as its conversion event. When they are not, say why, and do not quietly fall back to link clicks or invent an eligibility threshold.

- Ad creation owns the campaign plan, creative, previews, policy checks, creation and publishing, and their approvals. This setup never creates, publishes or spends on a campaign.

- When ad creation cannot open, say so and give the supported next step without claiming a campaign exists.

## Monitor setup health

- To confirm an existing connection is still live, such as when the merchant believes the store is already connected, read `shopify meta-app status` for the store, as "Inspect what already exists" describes. For a full health check, also run the standard dataset and catalog checks in [diagnosis](/opt/hatch/connectors/meta_ads/references/signals-connections-diagnosis.md), because the status alone does not show whether products sync or events arrive. When confirming it, name what the store is connected to in one line: its business portfolio, catalog and dataset, by the names from the Meta Business Manager reads rather than IDs or a generic label. Name only the reads you ran, by what each read returns, such as the product feed list for `ads_catalog_list_product_feeds`; never use the name of a related read you did not run, such as feed upload sessions or event-source health, and never report a check you did not run.

- Offer an ongoing setup check as its own decision, separate from setup and ad spend, and agree it before scheduling anything. In the offer, name what you would check: the Facebook & Instagram app's installation and authorization, the business portfolio, catalog and dataset connections, catalog diagnostics and Purchase event receipt. Name what counts as a problem: the app needing reauthorizing, a connection dropping, catalog errors, or Purchase events stopping while orders come in. Say you would check weekly and report in this chat. Then ask one yes or no question about running that weekly check in this chat, separate from any offer to start a sales ad. When you add options, offer one yes option and one no option rather than only yes options, and do not ask for the frequency in the same question. Do not record the connection or its status in memory; the weekly check reads it fresh.

- Schedule an approved check with `cron.add` as a read-only task covering the installation status, the business portfolio, catalog and dataset connections, catalog diagnostics and Purchase event receipt. Once real purchases arrive, it also checks that Purchase events carry value, currency and product IDs and that browser and server copies of the same purchase are counted once. Reuse a matching scheduled check if one exists, and confirm the schedule is active before calling it set up.

- A period with no purchases is not a fault on its own, and a failed or denied read is unknown, not healthy. Flag a confirmed fault with the affected connection and the supported fix.

- A detected problem never authorizes a repair, an asset change or a campaign change. Ask the merchant first.
